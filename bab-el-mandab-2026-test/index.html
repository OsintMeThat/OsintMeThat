On 9 October 2026, Saudi Arabia's ambassador to Yemen wrote on X that Bab al-Mandab had been "cleared of the remnants of the Houthi militia" (his post, 9 Oct[3]), as AFP reported the next day (AFP via Mereja, 10 Oct[4]). The same day, Asharq Al-Awsat reported, on unnamed military sources, that government forces held the strait and Mayun island (Asharq Al-Awsat, 9 Oct[5]). The next day, the government's own military sources told AFP that the strait was not secured (The New Arab, 10 Oct[6]). Five weeks later, on 15 November, the southern forces left the peninsula to defend Aden (Al-Monitor, 15 Nov[7]), and two Houthi videos toured what they had left behind on the road.

This article puts the announcements next to the ground: every government-side video posted from this front between September and November that could be geolocated, and the two Houthi videos filmed after the withdrawal, gone through vehicle by vehicle. The furthest line the government forces can be shown to have reached runs for about 12 km across the north and east of the peninsula, and every geolocated government video sits within about 1.3 km of it. At least 60 vehicles were left behind, at least 41 of them armored, and most of them lie less than a kilometre from that line.

Every figure here is a minimum. It covers only the footage that could be geolocated and what the two videos show.

Every vehicle and every geolocation in this article are also on one interactive map[8], each with its frame or its proof.

The announcements

In September the Houthis took the Red Sea coast down to the strait, mostly as government forces pulled back. Mocha fell on 9-10 September and the Houthis reached Dhubab the next day (Al Jazeera, 10 Sep[9]; Al Jazeera, 11 Sep[10]). On 14 September a Houthi video reposted via @BashaReport[11] claimed them on the peninsula itself. It geolocates south of the Bab al-Mandab lagoon, with the camera at (12.689437, 43.472063).

On 5 October the government answered with Operation Fajr al-Yemen, "Dawn of Yemen". The Giants and the Nation's Shield attacked around Dhubab with Saudi air support, the coalition speaking of 100 jets, and took the airport (Al Jazeera, 5 Oct[14]; The National, 5 Oct[15]). GeoConfirmed placed government forces at the airport that day (GeoConfirmed[16]). The same day the government said it held Mocha, Dhubab district and the strait itself, which its own sources speaking to AFP did not confirm beyond the airfield and the cut road (BBC, 6 Oct[17]). The strait was then announced cleared on 9 October, and denied the next day.

Where the offensive stopped

This is the part the footage can actually answer.

On 9 October a government camera at the eastern foot of the hills, at (12.699541, 43.537938), filmed strikes on Houthi positions inside them (geolocation, 9 Oct[18]). Three videos posted from 19 to 21 October show government fighters "engaging Houthi fighters entrenched on the heights". All three geolocate to the north edge of the peninsula, at (12.737704, 43.501660), then (12.729493, 43.500232) and (12.730210, 43.499586) (proof of 19 Oct[19]; proof of 20 Oct[23]; proof of 21 Oct[27]). On 27 October the Nation's Shield and the Giants claimed several hills (Asharq Al-Awsat, 27 Oct[31]), and that footage geolocates to (12.734811, 43.488422), still on the north edge (proof of 27 Oct[32]). On 2 November a video shows fighters walking upright, with no visible fire, at (12.730428, 43.499029): the same stretch of road that was fought over in October (proof of 2 Nov[36]).

In a month, the geolocated government footage moved by a few hundred metres.

The government also claimed the al-Ardhi camp on 22 October and the al-Jabzi camp, "east of Dhubab district", on 2 November (Sahafah24, 22 Oct[41]; Sawt al-Asima, 2 Nov[42]). Neither camp could be located, and none of the geolocated footage puts government forces beyond the line below.

The line

The line on the map, drawn from these geolocations, the positions of the wrecks and the terrain, is the furthest the government forces can be shown to have reached. It is an approximate maximum, not a front held every day. It runs for about 12 km: from the west coast north of the lagoon, across the main road, along the north edge of the hills, then down their eastern foot to the south coast.

Every government video geolocated between 9 October and 2 November sits on its north-east side, between 0.2 and 1.3 km from it. The least certain part is the western end, about 2 km from the nearest placed video. The forces may have pushed further at times, but no footage found shows it.

On 13 November, two days before the withdrawal was announced, the Giants filmed inside Dhubab airport to deny they were leaving (Sahafah24, 13 Nov[43]). The video geolocates inside the airport, at (12.863567, 43.520483), about 14 km behind the line.

What was left on the road

On 15 November the Houthi military media published a ground tour from the outskirts of Dhubab to Bab al-Mandab (video 1[47]). Drone footage of the same area was posted on 16 November (video 2[2]). Each vehicle in them is counted once across the two (vehicle register).

  • At least 60 vehicles, 19 of them seen in both videos.
  • At least 41 armored. 36 are matched to a model: 35 confirmed, one not sure. Five more are armored, but their model can't be read.
  • At least 12 more, pickups, technicals and a wheel loader, are set aside. Some may be Houthi vehicles: it is the least certain part of the count. At least seven more shapes are too far away to type.

Most of the identified models are built in the UAE: Streit, Harrow, NIMR and INKAS designs make up 23 of the 36. The seven US-made Oshkosh M-ATVs most likely came from UAE stocks, as Amnesty and CNN documented with the same forces in 2019 (Amnesty International, 6 Feb 2019[48]; CNN, Feb 2019[49]). The UAE was still shipping armored vehicles to Yemen at the end of 2025. On 30 December the Saudi-led coalition struck a shipment unloaded at Mukalla from Fujairah, whose vehicles Abu Dhabi said were "for use by the UAE forces operating in Yemen". The same day, the UAE said it would withdraw its remaining troops from the country (AP via PBS, 30 Dec 2025[50]; footage of the strike, 30 Dec 2025[51]). Many of the vehicles those forces left behind most likely passed to the Yemeni units they had worked with. Every model is identified by its shape alone: no plate or marking ties a vehicle to a unit or a buyer.

Where they lie says more than the count. Each position is fixed on stable ground, a road, a shore, a hillside, on imagery older than the fighting, never on the wreck itself. Of the 58 geolocated, 51 sit inside about 15 km², in six groups none wider than 1.3 km. Eleven lie within about 360 m of each other at a container roadblock on the main road, 570 m north of the line. Twelve lie within about 750 m on a beach by a stranded ship. Of the 54 with a known spot, 39 lie within 1 km of the line and 52 within 2 km. The wrecks are where the advance stopped.

When they were lost is harder to say. A Houthi video posted on 14 October already shows many of these vehicles, including #7 nose down in a ditch, #27 stopped beside it and a vehicle burning where #26 lies (14 Oct[52]). Those were lost by mid-October at the latest. For the others, where they lie makes it likely that most were lost during the offensive and the pushes that followed, not in the withdrawal. That is a reading of their positions, not a date.

24 of the 36 identified vehicles are destroyed. The other twelve were damaged or abandoned, five of them apparently salvageable. One M-ATV (#43) sits bogged at the surf line, still chained to a Tygor (#44) sunk to its axles: what looks like a tow that failed. Replacing everything filmed would cost at least $8.6M, and up to about $20M with every price at its high end (vehicle register).

What it says

Three things stand out, and none of them rests on a guess.

  • The announcements ran ahead of the ground. The strait was announced taken on 5 October and cleared on 9 October. No geolocated footage shows government forces beyond the line, south-west of which lie the lagoon, Bab al-Mandab village and the shore of the strait.
  • The front barely moved for a month. From 19 October to 2 November the government videos sit on the same few hundred metres of the north edge.
  • The losses sit on that line. Most of the vehicles lie less than a kilometre from where the advance stopped, and the ground was handed back on 15 November.

What the footage cannot say is why the offensive stalled, or how many men it cost. The Southern Armed Forces called the withdrawal a "redeployment", giving priority to securing Aden (Al-Monitor, 15 Nov[7]). Three military sources tied it to a Houthi flanking move through al-Madaribah, which from 10 November threatened the supply lines to Dhubab, Kahbub and the strait (Yemen Monitor, 16 Nov[54]). With the Houthis pushing on that flank, the forces on the peninsula risked being encircled: the withdrawal looks forced more than chosen. That is an assessment, not a statement from either side.

Method and limits

  • Geolocations. Each video is matched to the terrain on satellite imagery. The coordinates are the camera's position unless said otherwise. Dates are posting dates: the filming may be earlier.
  • The line. Drawn by hand from the geolocations, the positions of the wrecks and the imagery. It is an approximate maximum, good to a few hundred metres where the videos sit, and less sure at its western end, about 2 km from the nearest one.
  • Vehicles. Models are identified by shape against maker, press and Commons photos. The grade rates the model, not the loss. A position is where a vehicle was filmed: some may have been moved. The count covers only the two videos.
  • Dates of loss. The Houthi video reposted on 14 October already shows many of the vehicles, lost by then at the latest. For the others, a loss during the offensive is a reading of their positions.
  • Tools. The geolocations, maps and charts were made with Azimut[55] 0.3.4, in development, a free, open-source OSINT workbench that runs on your own computer.
